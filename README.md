A.R.E.S. is a small autonomous planetary rover project that we are building for the Stardance Challenge hosted by Hack Club and NASA. The main idea behind this build is to create a compact, reliable robotic platform capable of navigating unknown planetary terrain, mapping landscapes, and scanning for local fauna or interesting physical features during simulated space exploration missions. Instead of just making a basic remote-controlled car, we wanted to build something that feels like a real scientific research tool operating on a distant planet.

Because carrying a heavy AI processor directly on a small rover isn't really practical, we designed a Hybrid Edge AI system to split the workload efficiently. All the heavy computer vision logic—like object detection, obstacle mapping, and pathfinding using YOLOv8—is handled on a nearby host PC. The physical rover itself acts as a nimble remote unit. An ESP32-S3 microcontroller manages all the hardware operations on the ground: it streams live video from an OV2640 camera straight to the PC over Wi-Fi, drives the tracks, displays live telemetry data on a small ST7789 TFT screen, and even gives mission status reports out loud through a MAX98357 I2S audio amplifier and speaker.

On the physical side, the rover relies on a durable tracked chassis powered by DC motors and a dedicated motor driver, giving it enough traction to climb over small obstacles and uneven ground. Everything is powered by a custom 2S Li-ion battery pack, complete with a BMS protection board and buck converters to supply clean voltage to the sensors and microcontrollers. 

I am building and coding this project alongside my teammate Shabdan. We are tackling the whole engineering process together—from CAD design and 3D printing the chassis parts to wiring the electronics, writing the ESP32 firmware, and tuning the Python AI code on the PC side.
